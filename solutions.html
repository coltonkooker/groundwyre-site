Built around the job, not a generic dashboard

Every role touching a capital project — from the corner office to the field — gets a view of Groundwyre built for what they're actually accountable for.

Primary audience

City Manager

Renata Ibarra oversees a city of roughly 45,000–150,000 residents. She reports to an elected council and is personally accountable for how public money is spent and defended.

Goals
Deliver visible progress on infrastructure without blowing the budget or losing council trust. Reduce the number of "why didn't we fix this sooner" conversations at public meetings.
Frustrations
Department heads bring recommendations in incompatible formats. She's often the last to know when a project is behind or over budget. Constituent complaints outpace the city's ability to explain what's already planned.
What Groundwyre gives her
A single, defensible view of the capital plan she can bring into a council meeting — one that shows the trade-offs, not just the ask.
Primary audience

Public Works Director

Marcus Chen runs day-to-day operations across roads, water, and facilities. He manages field crews and a small planning staff, often with legacy asset management software from a decade ago.

images/photos/construction-equipment.jpg
Goals
Keep critical infrastructure functional with a budget that never fully covers the backlog. Prioritize the truly urgent repairs over the loudest complaints.
Frustrations
His current system tracks assets but doesn't help him decide what to fix first. Council asks for justifications he has to build manually every budget cycle.
What Groundwyre gives him
A prioritization engine grounded in real condition data, so he can defend a repaving schedule with evidence instead of instinct.
Primary audience

Transportation Planner

Devon Ashworth works within a transportation or MPO-adjacent department, focused on multi-year corridor planning, safety data, and grant-funded projects.

images/photos/highway-interchange-dusk.jpg
Goals
Build data-backed cases for federal and state grant applications. Balance safety, congestion, and equity considerations across competing corridor projects.
Frustrations
Grant applications require data pulled from multiple disconnected systems. Community input on transportation projects is often informal and hard to defend in a funding application.
What Groundwyre gives them
A way to combine traffic, safety, and engagement data into a single, exportable narrative that strengthens grant applications.
Secondary audience

Economic Development Director

Priya Nathan is focused on attracting investment and development to the city or county, often working across departments to align infrastructure readiness with growth plans.

Goals
Show developers and site selectors that infrastructure — roads, utilities, broadband — can support new growth on a credible timeline.
Frustrations
Infrastructure readiness data lives entirely inside public works, disconnected from the economic development conversation. She's often unable to answer a developer's infrastructure question without a multi-day internal request.
What Groundwyre gives her
Visibility into infrastructure capacity and planned upgrades she can reference directly in development conversations.
